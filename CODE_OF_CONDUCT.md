# Code of Conduct

Sigvault is built and maintained by people. We treat each other as
people.

## In one sentence

Be the colleague you wish you had.

## Concretely

- **Disagree on technical merit, not on identity.**
- **Assume the simplest interpretation that makes the other person
  competent and well-intentioned.** "I don't understand why you did X"
  beats "Why on earth did you do X?"
- **Reviews are about the change, not the contributor.**
- **Lurkers are allowed.** Reading without commenting is a valid mode
  of participation.
- **Newcomers get the benefit of the doubt.** "RTFM" is not a review
  comment; if a newcomer asks something the docs cover, the
  appropriate response is a link to the doc — and a PR if the doc
  could be clearer.
- **Beginners ship as-is, juniors ship with help, seniors ship with
  patience.**

## Specifically not allowed

- Personal attacks, insults, slurs, harassment of any kind.
- Public outing of private communications.
- Sustained disruption of issues, PRs, or chat.
- Sexual or violent content in project spaces.
- Doxing, stalking, threats.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness behaviour that breaks this code, write
to `conduct@sigvault.example` (placeholder until v4.3.0). Reports are
confidential. We will:

1. Acknowledge within 72 hours.
2. Investigate without naming the reporter.
3. Decide on action proportional to the incident — from a private
   conversation to a permanent ban from project spaces.

If a maintainer is the subject of a report, a different maintainer
handles it.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may lock issues, edit comments, ban accounts from project
spaces (issues, PRs, discussions, future chat). Decisions are
documented in the issue tracker (or, for sensitive cases, in a private
log).

## Scope

This code applies to all project spaces — repository, issues, pull
requests, future Discord/Matrix, conferences where Sigvault is
represented, and one-on-one communication arising from project
business.

## Inspired by

- The Linux kernel's "Code of Conduct" (2018 onwards) — for the
  emphasis on professional conduct over identity politics.
- The Rust project's "Code of Conduct" — for the practical reporting
  workflow.
- Django's "Code of Conduct" — for the welcoming-newcomers framing.
